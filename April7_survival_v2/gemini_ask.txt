Okay, that's a great specific question! How someone should list items depends on how much detail is needed for the help they're asking for.
Here are a few ways, from simple to more detailed, along with when you might use them:
1. Simple List (Good for Brainstorming/Basic Concepts)
If you just want me to be aware of the items or incorporate their names into a story or description, a simple list is fine:
I want these items in the game:
- Healing Potion
- Rusty Sword
- Skeleton Key
- Map Fragment
- Magic Amulet

2. List with Basic Function/Description (Better for General Design/Story)
If you want the items to have a bit more context or a basic role defined:
Here are the items and their basic purpose:
- Healing Potion: Restores some health.
- Rusty Sword: A basic weapon for fighting.
- Skeleton Key: Opens any simple lock.
- Map Fragment: Part of a larger map needed to find a location.
- Magic Amulet: Protects slightly against magic attacks.

3. Structured List with Details (Best for Mechanics, Rules, Code Logic)
This is generally the most helpful format, especially if you want help designing interactions, rules, or code snippets involving these items. Provide key details for each item.
Here are the specific items I want included:

* **Item Name:** Healing Potion
    * **Description:** A small vial containing a swirling red liquid that smells faintly of cinnamon.
    * **Function/Effect:** Restores 25 health points when used. Consumed on use.
    * **Location (Optional):** Found in chests, dropped by weak monsters, sold by merchants.

* **Item Name:** Rusty Sword
    * **Description:** An old, short sword, pitted with rust but still serviceable.
    * **Function/Effect:** Melee weapon. Deals 5-8 damage. Can be equipped.
    * **Location (Optional):** Starting equipment, found near the beginning area.

* **Item Name:** Skeleton Key
    * **Description:** An ornate key made of blackened bone.
    * **Function/Effect:** Unlocks any door with a 'Simple Lock'. Breaks after 3 uses.
    * **Location (Optional):** Found in the dungeon master's quarters.

* **Item Name:** Map Fragment (North)
    * **Description:** A torn piece of old parchment showing the northern part of the Whispering Woods.
    * **Function/Effect:** Quest item. Needs to be combined with other fragments (South, East, West) to reveal the path to the hidden temple.
    * **Location (Optional):** Dropped by the goblin leader.

* **Item Name:** Amulet of Warding
    * **Description:** A simple bronze amulet on a leather cord, glowing faintly.
    * **Function/Effect:** Provides +5 resistance to magical damage when equipped in the amulet slot.
    * **Location (Optional):** Reward for completing the 'Help the Alchemist' quest.

Why the Structured Format is Often Best:
 * Clear Name: Identifies the item unambiguously.
 * Description: Helps me write flavour text, or understand its visual/lore context.
 * Function/Effect: This is crucial! It tells me what the item does, which is necessary for designing game mechanics, rules, or code related to its use (e.g., if player uses 'Healing Potion': player_health += 25).
 * Optional Details (Location, Stats, etc.): Add depth and help integrate the item into the game world and systems more effectively.
In summary:
 * For simple mentions: Basic List.
 * For story/description context: List with Basic Function/Description.
 * For designing mechanics, rules, interactions, or code logic: Structured List with Details is strongly preferred.
The more detail you provide in a structured way, the better I can understand your vision and help you incorporate those items meaningfully into the game concept or design.
